# Model card: ML demand forecaster

## Overview

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Model name | `demand_forecaster` |
| Version format | `mlp-quantile-1.0.0` |
| Type | Supervised regression, multi-quantile |
| Framework | PyTorch |
| Owner | M3 Forecast/Reorder |
| Status | Demo / challenger. Falls back to the rule-based baseline whenever it cannot serve safely. |

## Intended use

- Estimate daily demand for perishable SKUs in a single small shop or bakery.
- Feed the median (`q50`) into `ReorderService` for buy/bake/hold/markdown recommendations.
- Expose `q10`–`q90` as an 80% uncertainty range for the approval screen.
- Support what-if demand estimates when a holiday or promotion override is present.

The model is decision support only. A person approves every action that costs money or changes production.

## Not intended for

- Automatic purchasing, baking, pricing, markdown, donation, or write-off.
- Food-safety decisions. Expired stock must never be sold.
- Multi-store, multi-warehouse, or cross-chain forecasting.
- Products or categories absent from training without a baseline fallback.
- Long-horizon planning beyond a few lead-time/review days.
- Certified SDG, CO2e, or meal-equivalent accounting.

## Training data

- Deterministic synthetic history generated by `forecast/ml/catalog.py`.
- Default: 60 SKUs derived from the 8 base products, 730 days, weekday and annual seasonality, trend, holidays, promotions, and censored stockouts.
- Each training example uses history up to the day before the target date.
- Stockout-censored days are excluded as targets, and lag gaps are forward-filled instead of being treated as zero demand.
- The real `SalesObservation` contract can replace the synthetic generator without changing the model.

## Inputs

- Per-SKU daily demand history with stockout-censoring flags.
- Product metadata: category, domain, shelf life, lead time, MOQ, safety-stock days.
- Calendar overrides: date, multiplier, optional SKU.
- Target date and anchor date.

## Outputs

- Three quantiles per target day: `q10`, `q50`, `q90`.
- The `MLForecaster` converts these into `DailyForecast` rows with lower/upper bounds, an `error_std` estimate, confidence, reason codes, and a plain-language `why` list.

## Training procedure

- Time-based train/validation split (last 20% of target dates).
- Adam optimizer, pinball loss, early stopping on validation pinball.
- Numeric standardization fitted on the training split only.
- Checkpoint contains the state dict, feature encoder, model config, metrics, and training timestamp.

## Evaluation

Primary metric: WAPE of the median forecast. Secondary: pinball loss, bias, and 80% interval coverage. Compare against `BaselineForecaster` on the same validation examples.

Promotion gate:

- WAPE no more than 1 point worse than baseline.
- Bias within 10% of mean actual demand.
- 80% coverage within 72–88%.

A failed run remains registered as `challenger`; it does not become `champion`.

## Sample results

Default synthetic run (`--days 730 --products 60 --epochs 40`):

| Metric | ML | Baseline |
|---|---|---|
| WAPE | 13.3% | 13.1% |
| Bias | -0.68 units | 0.21 units |
| 80% coverage | 83.6% | n/a |
| Validation examples | 8,363 | 8,363 |

These numbers validate the pipeline. They are not evidence of real-world accuracy.

## Limitations and risks

- Synthetic data may overstate how predictable demand is.
- The baseline trailing mean is strong on this generator; the ML gain is small.
- The 80% interval is only checked on one holdout split.
- One global model can underfit unusual local products.
- New products fall back to the baseline until they appear in a retrained vocabulary.
- Promotions and holidays are learned as a multiplier feature, not as causal effects.
- Quantile crossing is prevented by sorting at inference, not by the loss itself.

## Fallback and safety

Fallback to `BaselineForecaster` occurs on missing/unloadable checkpoints, unseen SKUs, missing or stale history, and fewer than 14 valid days. The result is marked `ML_FALLBACK_BASELINE` and keeps the baseline reason codes. Recommendations still require human approval, and stock movements remain the only way quantities change.

## Monitoring

- Track WAPE, bias, and 80% coverage by week and by category.
- Track fallback rate and `ML_LOW_COVERAGE` rate.
- Compare champion ML against the baseline on the same dates.
- Retrain when product mix or calendar behaviour changes materially.
- Never promote on in-sample metrics.

## Ethical and sustainability notes

CO2e and meal-equivalent figures are estimates. Report the emission factors, baseline policy, and assumptions alongside any SDG claim. The model should reduce over-ordering and waste, not justify understocking essential food.
